PHOENIX — CHANGES AND FIXES
===========================

Newest first. "Windows" is the Windows app, "Mac/Linux" the Electron app for macOS and Linux,
"Android" the phone app. Where a line doesn't name one, it applies to Windows and Mac/Linux.


1.0.7 — 2026-11-27
------------------

New
- Find a setting: a box above the settings categories finds any option by its words ("equalizer",
  "theme", "pairing"), across every page; choosing one opens its page and points it out. Android:
  settings → "find a setting".

Changed
- Listening stats under a minute read in seconds ("36 seconds listened") instead of 0 minutes.

Fixed
- Windows: an episode's picture is no longer cut off when it isn't a widescreen frame.


1.0.6 — 2026-11-27
------------------

New
- Keyboard shortcuts: Ctrl + Up / Down for the volume, Ctrl + Shift + Right / Left to jump ten
  seconds, Ctrl + L to like the song playing (again to unlike). F1 lists every shortcut.
  (⌘ instead of Ctrl on a Mac.)
- The phone page can turn phone sync on right there, shows the paired phones and when each last
  synced, and says how to pair one when none is.
- The welcome page (nothing in the collection yet) adds your usual Music, Videos and Pictures
  folders in one click, and scans them.
- A search that finds nothing says so, and how else to look.

Changed
- Messages in the title bar (Android: under the title) fade after ten seconds instead of staying
  until the next one. Messages about work still going on ("scanning…") stay while it goes on.
- A song list beside the albums that mixes several albums (all of an artist, all artists, a genre)
  shows each song's album, and its artist when there are several, after the title, so two songs
  called the same can be told apart.
- Stretches under a minute read "48 seconds" instead of "0 minutes".

Fixed
- Windows: when Phoenix starts with nothing playing, the player bar's song area stays empty
  instead of showing "nothing playing" until the first song.
- Windows: podcast episode names no longer start with two empty boxes, and a podcast without a
  picture shows a microphone instead of a blank square.
- Windows: the note at the top of the file tags window lines up with the buttons under it.


1.0.5 — 2026-11-26
------------------

New
- Pairing a phone is one step. On the phone, settings → sync with PC → "pair with my pc" finds
  the computer and asks it; Phoenix there comes to the front and asks whether to allow the phone.
  No code to type.
- A QR code in the computer's settings (settings → phone → sync options): point the phone's camera
  at it and open the link, and Phoenix opens on the phone and pairs by itself. If Phoenix isn't on
  the phone yet, the page offers to download it.
- Each phone gets a key of its own. The computer's settings list the paired phones (when each
  last synced and what it did) and can forget one without the others having to pair again. The
  six-digit pairing code still works, also for phones with an older Phoenix.
- Android: the phone syncs by itself whenever Phoenix is opened and the computer can be reached
  (at most every ten minutes; can be turned off). Music added to the phone sync list on the
  computer arrives on the phone without tapping anything.
- Android: when the computer's address changes (routers hand out new ones), the phone finds it
  again by itself.
- While a phone syncs, the computer's status line shows what it is doing ("Pixel 8: copying 3 of
  40"), and when it is done.

Changed
- Android: the phone looks for the computer on each of its networks' own broadcast addresses as
  well, which some routers pass on when they block the general one.
- Android: clearer messages when the computer can't be found or has forgotten the phone.

Fixed
- Windows: rows of buttons in settings (such as the update buttons) wrap onto another line
  instead of running off the edge of a narrow window.


1.0.4 — 2026-11-26
------------------

New
- Windows: "Add to phone sync list" when you right-click songs, albums, artists or playlists
  (besides dragging them to the phone icon).

Changed
- The two kinds of tags are told apart: "Edit file tags…" (title, artist, genre and so on,
  saved into the music files; it was "Tag editor") and "Edit search tags (#)…" (Phoenix's own
  labels, kept in its library and found by search as #tag; it was "Edit tags"). Each window says
  which kind it is and where its tags are kept.


1.0.3 — 2026-11-26
------------------

Changed
- Windows: updates install by themselves. INSTALL (settings → updates) downloads the new version,
  runs its installer with no wizard to click through and starts Phoenix again when it's done.
  Going back one version works the same way.
- Windows: a portable copy (unpacked from the zip) updates itself as well: it fetches the new
  zip, puts its files in place while it runs and starts again. Updating by itself and going back
  one version work for portable copies too.
- Mac/Linux and Android: no changes besides the version number (released together with Windows).


1.0.2 — 2026-11-26
------------------

New
- Auto equalizer (settings → playback, on Windows, Mac/Linux and Android): each song is measured once
  (how much of its sound falls in each of the ten bands) and the equalizer is set for it as it
  plays, on top of your own equalizer. Three sounds: sound accuracy (an even tonal balance),
  clarity (voices and detail forward, muddiness trimmed) and bass (a fuller low end, more on thin
  songs, less on bass-heavy ones). Measurements are kept in Phoenix's library, in each song's tags
  (so they travel with the file, also to your phone) or in a phoenix-eq.xml file in each album
  folder. Songs are measured the first time they play, or all at once.
- Auto equalizer, AI part: Google's YAMNet sound-recognition model recognises each song's style
  (electronic, hip hop, rock, pop, acoustic, jazz, classical; singing or speech) so it's evened
  out towards a balance that suits it. It runs on the graphics card on Windows (DirectML, any AMD,
  Intel or NVIDIA graphics), Core ML on Macs, NNAPI on Android and the processor on Linux. The
  model (15 MB) is downloaded the first time it's turned on.
- Headphones and speakers: pick yours from the AutoEq project's measurements of thousands of
  models, and its correction goes on top of everything else the equalizer does.
- Android: tabs, links and tile rows wrap onto another line instead of scrolling sideways.
- Background picture: settings → display can put a picture of your own behind the window, showing
  through the theme's background as much as you choose (5–90%). Phoenix keeps its own copy in the
  data folder.
- The player bar's song, cover and progress hide while nothing is playing.
- Now playing: a "generated" tab beside lyrics shows the lyrics the speech model wrote, kept apart
  from a song's own lyrics. It is there while a speech model is downloaded and chosen.
- Now playing: settings → display can put a panel behind the song list and lyrics, so they stay
  readable over the visualizer.
- Lyrics written by a speech model on this computer (whisper.cpp): it listens to a song and writes
  what is sung, with timing, line by line as it hears it. Nothing is sent anywhere. It runs on any
  AMD, Intel or NVIDIA graphics, built in or separate (Vulkan), on Apple chips (Metal), or on the
  processor; you can choose which. Six models from 75 MB to 2.9 GB, with the one that suits your
  graphics card's memory marked as recommended. It can write lyrics as songs play, or for every
  song in the collection.
- Ripping (Windows): FLAC (lossless), and MP3 at 128/192/256/320 kbps, AAC at 128/192/256 kbps and
  WMA at 128/192 kbps, besides WAV.
- Burning (Windows): an MP3 CD (hours of music for MP3 CD players and car stereos; songs that
  aren't MP3s are converted at the quality you choose) or a data disc (the files as they are, on
  a CD, DVD or Blu-ray), besides an audio CD. Files are numbered in burn list order with a
  playlist, and the burn list shows the size and when a DVD or Blu-ray is needed.
- Themes: "follow the system" can use a theme of your own for the system's light side and another
  for its dark side.
- Android: lyrics in now playing. Tap "lyrics" on the picture: from a .lrc or .txt file beside the
  song, its tags, LRCLIB (found by itself if settings → lyrics says so) or the clipboard. Timed
  lyrics follow the song, and tapping a line jumps there.
- Android: settings → playback has an equalizer (the desktop's presets, or ten bands of your own,
  set on the phone's own equalizer) and volume levelling from ReplayGain tags, by song or by album.
- Android: the theme can follow the phone's light or dark setting.

Changed
- The note under the lyrics wraps instead of being cut off, in larger, brighter type.
- Listening for lyrics avoids whisper's habit of repeating a line, splits long sentences into
  lyric-sized lines, uses the song's name as a hint, and weighs several readings on a GPU.
- The installers and packages carry only their own platform's speech-model libraries.

Fixed
- Android: setting titles that were cut off on narrow screens are shorter.
- Mac/Linux: asking for generated lyrics a song doesn't have no longer reports an error.


1.0.1 — 2026-11-26
------------------

New
- Keep running in the notification area (Windows) / tray or menu bar (Mac/Linux) when the window
  is closed. Off by default.
- A built-in "system" theme that follows the system's light or dark setting; the title bar follows
  the theme.
- Custom themes, and a designer to make them inside Phoenix.
- Streaming services can go anywhere in the collection, and tabs of your own hold them (set up
  under settings → servers → streaming).
- Mac/Linux caught up with Windows: collection tabs, radio, podcasts and their downloads, sorting
  and filtering, stats, scrobbling, sleep timer, speed, backup, updates, streaming services, tag
  editor, Smart DJ, auto playlists, library care, metadata sources, lyrics, artist photos,
  Mixview, crossfade, equalizer, volume levelling, mini player, media keys, themes, casting (DLNA
  and Chromecast), phone remote, translated names, and moving the data folder.
- Android: settings and pages arranged like the desktop's; radio, podcast sources, sleep timer,
  speed, stats, scrobbling, sorting and filtering, backup and update check. Builds are signed with
  a fixed key.

Fixed
- Mac/Linux: a broken message in settings → storage.
- Windows: the audio client is released properly; internet pictures load on their own lane;
  volume levelling no longer holds the audio lock.
- Radio plays https streams (through the audio engine).

- First release: the Windows app, the Android app and the Mac/Linux app, with a shared core.
- Smooth scrolling at the display's refresh rate, cover grids and the visualizer drawn with
